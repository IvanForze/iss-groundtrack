Orbital Mechanics

Why does the ISS never pass on the same path twice?

🌍

1. Earth Rotates Underneath (~22.9° Westward Shift)

The ISS takes approximately 92.6 minutes to complete one full revolution in space. While the ISS completes 1 orbit, the Earth spins eastward by roughly 23 degrees of longitude (~2,550 km) beneath it. Therefore, each subsequent pass is shifted westward across a completely new region!

📐

2. 51.6° Orbital Inclination

The ISS orbit is tilted at 51.6 degrees to Earth's equator. This creates the characteristic sinusoidal ground track waveform visible on the 2D map, covering areas between 51.6° N and 51.6° S latitude.

🔄

3. Nodal Precession (Earth's Oblateness)

Earth is not a perfect sphere; its equatorial bulge creates a gravitational perturbation that slowly rotates the orbital plane westward by about 5 degrees per day.
